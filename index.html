READER NOTE 000A Warning About Words

Do not mistake this book for paper and ink.

A screen can show you a thousand images and leave nothing behind.

A book asks you to create the image yourself.

When you read the word ocean, there is no ocean on this page. And yet—you can see it. You can hear it. You may even smell the salt.

Write coffee, and somewhere inside you a cup becomes warm. Write rain, and an old street may suddenly become wet again. Write the name of someone you once loved—and an entire world can return.

This is the strange technology of words.

FILE 08-A / SECTION 08The Crime of Productive Leisure

The Department has received credible reports that citizens are optimising their rest.

You have a hobby, and the hobby has a spreadsheet. You go for a walk, and the walk has a target. You read a book faster because somewhere you absorbed the idea that books are objects to clear rather than countries in which to become temporarily lost.

Even sleep has become a performance review delivered each morning by a watch that did not sleep at all.

FILE 23-A / SECTION 23The Word the Age Removed

There is a word that has quietly disappeared from circulation.

No announcement accompanied its removal. There was no ceremony, no final use recorded in the archive. You simply reached for the word one day and found another word occupying its place.

The replacement was more.

The missing word is enough.

THE PERSON BEHIND THE DEPARTMENT

Meet Orion Saint.

Orion Saint is the writing name of Ivaylo Peytchev, a Bulgarian artist and writer based in Fort Lauderdale, Florida.

His life moves between painting, hands-on work, and driving through South Florida. Conversations on the road sit beside the questions that run through his art: how we live, what we obey, and how much of ourselves we give away.

In The Orion Protocol, those questions enter a fictional office full of recovered files, absurd instructions, and an officer beginning to question his own paperwork.
